How I film a ceremony

You can see both camera angles in Clayton & Isabelle's vows: a tighter one up front, off to the side, and a wide one from the back of the center aisle. Some churches only allow video from the back of the church. The published rules I've checked are further down this page.

For audio, mics go on the groom and the officiant, and I also record a backup on camera, so the sound is collected two ways. When a church doesn't allow mics on the wedding party, I rely on the on-camera recording.

I typically skip the rehearsal. We set your two hours around the ceremony on our planning call, and on the day I work alongside your planner and photographer. To see where the ceremony sits in a full day of coverage, read planning your wedding day timeline.

Church and chapel rules that decide where the cameras go

Here's what a few Oxford and Jackson metro churches and chapels publish about cameras and mics. It's not a list of places I've filmed, and policies change, so check your church's current rules too. Rules checked September 2026.

Oxford

Paris-Yates Chapel, Ole Miss

Cameras: "Balcony access is not permitted, even to photographers during the ceremony." The podiums and all other furniture are permanent and cannot be moved. Only the altar table and the large chairs on the stage can be moved. No tape of any kind may be used on the floors, including to mark positions.

Mics and sound: Two built-in microphones sit at the permanent podiums and are turned on by University staff for the rehearsal and the wedding. Music plays through the chapel's Bluetooth-compatible system from a cellphone, and the wedding party runs it.

Bells: They chime automatically on the hour and half-hour unless silenced by request, so ask for that if they would land during your vows.

Source: Paris-Yates Chapel weddings page

St. John the Evangelist Catholic Church

Cameras: The parish wedding guidelines (updated December 4, 2025) say photographers must consult the parish wedding coordinator in advance and may not enter the sanctuary near the altar.

Diocese policy: St. John's is in the Catholic Diocese of Jackson, whose policy (updated 2014) says photographers should never be in the sanctuary or near the altar before, during and after the ceremony.

Sources: St. John's wedding guidelines and Diocese of Jackson clergy resources

First Presbyterian Church of Oxford

Cameras: The church's wedding policy (revised August 26, 2021) allows photography and videography only from the rear of the church during the service. So both of my camera angles would be at the rear.

Source: First Presbyterian wedding policy

Jackson metro

St. Andrew's Episcopal Cathedral

Cameras and mics: Video may be recorded only from the back of the center aisle. TV monitors, lights and cabling of any kind are not allowed, and neither are microphones on the wedding party. Guests may not take photos or video from the pews.

Audio: With no mics allowed on the wedding party, your vows would come from my on-camera recording at the back of the center aisle.

Source: St. Andrew's photography and videography standards

First Baptist Jackson

Vendor guidelines: All wedding vendors must review and accept the church's vendor guidelines. Videographers have their own set, separate from the photographers', and I'd accept them before your wedding.

Source: First Baptist Jackson wedding planning page

Catholic parishes in the Jackson metro

Diocese policy: The same Diocese of Jackson policy as St. John's above applies here.

Source: Diocese of Jackson clergy resources

If your church isn't listed, ask the office for its wedding or vendor policy and send it along when you check your date.

Ceremony film questions

How long is the ceremony film?

The full ceremony film runs as long as your ceremony, usually 20 to 40 minutes, and the cut-down version goes in your video book.

Is the full ceremony just raw footage?

No, it's an edit, not a raw file. If you're asking because you want the raw footage, my raw footage policy is on my questions page.

Is the ceremony film in Legacy the same film?

Yes. It's the same ceremony film as the Ceremony package, bundled into Legacy's ten hours, so there's no need to book both.
