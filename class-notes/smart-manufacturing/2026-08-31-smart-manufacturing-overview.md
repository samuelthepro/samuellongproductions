## Summary (Granola AI notes)

### Smart Manufacturing and Industry 4.0

-   Smart manufacturing: traditional processes linked with advanced technologies for greater efficiency, flexibility, and profit
-   Industry 4.0 combines physical and digital worlds: machines and people working together
-   Extends beyond manufacturing: healthcare, agriculture, transportation
-   Industry 5.0 distinction: focused on humans and machines working together (vs. 4.0’s digital-physical convergence)

### Key Technologies Covered

-   IoT sensors: collect real-time machine data, feed AI algorithms for predictive alerts and cloud sharing
-   Cobots vs. robots:
    -   Robots: large, caged, dangerous if contact occurs
    -   Cobots: designed to work safely beside people, shut down on contact, handle repetitive or hazardous tasks
-   Digital twins: simulate process changes without taking the line down
-   Augmented reality: overlays instructions/manuals onto equipment for training and maintenance
-   Blockchain: digital supply chain tracking from raw material to customer
-   3D printing: on-demand precision components (aerospace, replacement parts)
-   Edge vs. cloud computing: edge makes decisions locally at the machine; cloud shares data across facilities

### Benefits of Smart Manufacturing

-   Predictive maintenance: sensors detect early failure signs (vibration, temperature spikes), replacing parts only when needed
    -   Old model: scheduled shutdowns twice a year, replacing parts regardless of condition
-   Defect detection: vision systems catch defects in milliseconds vs. random human sampling
-   Resource optimization: AI idles unused machines, redistributes workers, optimizes material cutting layouts
-   Flexibility: product changeovers in under 10 minutes vs. hours of manual retooling
-   Traceability: rapid recall response (e.g., Taylor Farms salmonella case used as example)
-   Dashboards: real-time KPIs, downtime analysis, production rates, defect rates, all configurable

### Challenges and Risks

-   High initial investment: smaller companies cannot convert all at once, may start with cobots only
-   Legacy system integration: old PLCs and SCADA systems very difficult to connect to new tech
    -   Winchester example: still running 1940s equipment that works, no business case to replace it
-   Cybersecurity: more connected devices mean more entry points, especially risky for newly connected small companies
-   Data volume: a single sensor generates millions of data points; scaling to a full factory is a major challenge
-   Skilled workforce shortage and regulatory/compliance complexity

### 5G and Future of IoT in Manufacturing

-   Private 5G networks: factories building their own to avoid shared-network latency
    -   5G latency under 1 millisecond, enabling real-time remote robot control
    -   Supports thousands of simultaneous sensors without straining the network
-   Edge AI: instant machine shutdowns on anomaly detection, no human delay or approval needed
-   Mobile cobots on AGVs: moving autonomously across production lines for parts delivery
-   AR for remote troubleshooting: technician can diagnose equipment from another floor or location
-   More AI-driven decision making expected as mid-size companies follow larger adopters
