Can you fly a drone at Ole Miss? Campus, the Grove and game day

Only with approval from UPD, the university police, and never near the stadium on a home football game day. I'm Samuel Long, an FAA Part 107 pilot in Oxford. I have a working relationship with UPD and I'm authorized to fly on campus, so I handle the approval for my clients. Here's what the University of Mississippi and the FAA say.

Flying on campus

Can I fly a drone on the Ole Miss campus?

Not without approval from UPD (University Police and Campus Safety). Every campus flight goes through UPD's UAS Request Form, which has to be approved "prior to any UAS operations on university property." That covers students, faculty and staff as well as hired pilots; the form asks every applicant for their affiliation. The flight also has to be for school, research or business, because the university's drone policy says hobby flying on campus is generally not permitted.

How do I get approval?

Submit the UAS Request Form to UPD. It asks for your Part 107 certificate number, the purpose of the flight, the exact location, the dates and times, the drone's model, weight and serial number, whether it's registered, and whether you'll take photos or video. Once it's approved, the operator has to carry a copy during the flight and show it to any university official who asks. The university's drone policy also requires FAA registration, with the registration number marked on the outside of the drone and matching the number on your request.

What if the flight involves Athletics?

You need a second approval. The form says any activity involving Athletics also needs Athletics' own approval, uploaded with your request. The university's drone policy goes further: you need Athletics' OK to fly at or over an Athletics venue, or even to record one. That covers Vaught-Hemingway Stadium, the Pavilion and Swayze Field, plus the university golf course and the practice fields.

How far ahead should I ask?

The university's drone policy says the request has to go in at least 48 hours before the flight. I submit campus requests at least a week ahead, and earlier when Athletics has to sign off too. A commercial film on campus also needs permission under the university's filming policy, and that request is due at least 30 days before the first day of filming.

Does the rule apply at chapter houses?

If the house sits on university property, yes. Many Ole Miss chapter houses do, so I check the address and file the request with UPD before a date party, a formal or a parents weekend at the house.

Game day, the Grove and the Square

Can you fly a drone over the Grove on game day?

No, for two reasons. The university's drone policy bans flying over crowds and names the Grove on game days, so flying over it is out the whole day, not just around kickoff. And FAA rules ban drone flights within 3 nautical miles of a stadium during NCAA Division I football games, from an hour before the game until an hour after it. That circle around Vaught-Hemingway Stadium covers the Grove, all of campus and the Square.

What about days without a home game?

The stadium restriction only applies around game time. On other days the Grove and the rest of campus still need UPD's approval, and the university's drone policy still bans flying over any crowd, including outdoor concerts and commencement. The Square isn't university property and its airspace is open, but I still need a legal spot to take off and land.

Do baseball and basketball games count?

Not under the FAA's standing stadium rule, which covers Division I football but not college baseball or basketball. Swayze Field and the Pavilion are Athletics venues, though, so the UPD and Athletics approvals still apply, and so does the university's ban on flying over crowds. Check the FAA's temporary flight restrictions before any flight too, since special events and VIP visits can add others.

Is Oxford's airspace controlled?

Not at drone altitudes. FAA airspace data puts Oxford, the campus and the Square in Class G airspace up to 700 feet, so a flight under 400 feet needs no FAA airspace authorization. University-Oxford Airport has no control tower.

If you're flying it yourself

I'm a student with a drone. What rules apply to me?

If you fly purely for fun, the FAA's recreational rules apply: pass the free TRUST test, register your drone if it weighs 250 grams or more, fly no higher than 400 feet and keep it where you can see it. Just not on campus. The university's drone policy says hobby flying there is generally not permitted, so campus flights are for school, research or business, and each one needs UPD's approval.

When do I need a Part 107 certificate?

Whenever the flight isn't purely for fun: if you're paid, or if the footage is for a business. That's the FAA's Part 107 rule (14 CFR 107.12), and the university's form asks for your certificate number. One exception on campus: the university's drone policy lets a student flying for a class, supervised by faculty, follow the FAA's recreational rules without a certificate.

Hiring a pilot for a campus shoot

Can you handle the approvals for our shoot?

Yes. I have a working relationship with UPD and I'm authorized to fly on campus. For each shoot I file the request with UPD, get Athletics' approval when the shoot needs it, check the FAA's restrictions for the date and carry the approved form on the day. If a spot or a date can't work, I'll tell you up front.

What does the university require from a hired pilot?

More than the request form. Under the university's drone policy, a hired pilot has to show proof of liability insurance, at least 1 million dollars per occurrence and 2 million overall, and a university representative has to be with the pilot for the whole flight. The university may charge fees, and hired pilots can't use its expedited same-day approval.

What do you shoot on campus?

Chapter events, campus organization events and brand films for businesses that want Ole Miss in the shot, each with UPD's approval. A brand film also needs permission under the university's filming policy, requested from University Marketing and Communications at least 30 days ahead. The university can't be identified in the film, from the Grove and campus buildings to its logos, without their written OK. My drone photography Q&A covers the rest of what I fly.
